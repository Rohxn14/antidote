# Scenario suite

Generated by `python -m scripts.scenario_suite` (21 named scenarios, 400 random cases, seed 7, 313 s on a laptop with local FalkorDB).

Every case runs through the real pipeline, then **a reviewer who approves everything without looking** presses Approve, so the results show what Antidote stops on its own. The right order is the cheapest supplier that **truly** qualifies (really certified, really delivers in time) and did not try to manipulate the agents. *Without Antidote* is the same agents reading the same documents with no checks, no review and no approval check.

The documents and the agents' answers are generated (see app/casebuilder.py): this measures Antidote's checks and gate across many cases, not a real model or real suppliers.

## 400 random cases

4 to 12 suppliers each; about 40% not really certified, a quarter of those claim a certificate anyway; 15% hide one of six published attacks in one of five ways (15% of those push another supplier); 7% claim to be the only certified supplier; 5% lie about delivery time; 8% of registry entries are wrong or missing; half of all pages carry ordinary hidden menus and banners.

|---|---|---|

| stopped: bad order held for a person | 60 | 0 |
| held: honest order waits | 24 | 0 |
| missed: no order | 2 | 0 |
| wrong: cannot deliver in time | 8 | 5 |
| wrong: not really certified | 6 | 129 |
| wrong: pays more than needed | 3 | 5 |
| wrong: rewarded a manipulator | 0 | 145 |

What caught the problems (cases where each check fired): conflict 320, hidden fact 268, scan 227, verification hold 84.
